Protecting Students in an Unfiltered Digital World

Nearly 3 in 4 young people have encountered harmful content online, according to Australia's eSafety Commissioner 1 . As schools worldwide rethink their digital safeguards and governments introduce social media restrictions, society is taking decisive action against these digital risks.

With clear risk visibility, prioritised resource allocation, and a proven duty-of-care framework, DWSF equips children, teachers, and parents with the tools and knowledge to navigate the digital world safely.

Our project is part of a growing shift from reactive crisis management to proactive digital resilience — helping schools move ahead of harm, not just respond to it. Together, we can build a safer online environment and foster responsible digital citizenship.

The DWSF Solution: 5-Pillar Benchmarking Framework

Our framework evaluates a school's digital safety maturity across 4 core operational pillars, bounded by an overarching governance structure.

1. Educated Child

Assesses student digital literacy, agency, clear reporting channels, and peer support systems. Ensures students can identify risks and safely ask for help.

2. Engaged Parent

Evaluates community outreach, family workshops, and transparent communication regarding home-to-school device rules and digital citizenship.

3. Empowered Users

Measures technical infrastructure, content filtering, clear staff escalation protocols, incident response readiness, and privacy protections.

4. Accessible Help

Evaluates easily reachable reporting tools, student support pathways, anonymous reporting mechanisms, and immediate crisis response channels.

5. Governance

Provides overarching executive leadership, policy frameworks, staff compliance training, and continuous legal duty-of-care alignment across all pillars.

About the Creator

Photo Placeholder

PS / Founder

Cybersecurity Professional & Online Safety Advocate

PS is a security professional with over two decades helping organisations enhance their physical and cyber protections.

As a parent, PS watched other parents grow increasingly concerned about how much time their children spend online.

That concern sparked an idea: combine traditional cybersecurity principles with psychology and apply them to online safety.

The result is a framework that helps schools build a Digital Wellbeing Program from scratch — or assess and strengthen an existing one — so protective measures can adapt and improve over time.
